1. Direct Stress & Strain Pages 1-2

Direct Stress

When force is applied on a body, it deforms. To resist deformation, internal resistance is offered by material per unit area. This is called Stress.

$$\sigma = \frac{F}{A}$$

Where: $\sigma$ = Stress, $F$ = Force, $A$ = Cross-sectional area

Units: N/m² or Pa. 1 N/mm² = 1 MN/m² = 10⁶ N/m²

Strain

When a bar is subjected to force, it elongates or shortens. Strain is the ratio of change in length to original length.

$$\varepsilon = \frac{\Delta L}{L}$$

Strain has no unit. It is linear strain or longitudinal strain.

2. Hooke's Law & Elastic Constants Pages 3-5

Hooke's Law: Within elastic limit, stress is directly proportional to strain.

$$\sigma \propto \varepsilon \quad \text{or} \quad \sigma = E \varepsilon$$

$E$ = Young's Modulus or Modulus of Elasticity

Modulus of Rigidity (G)

Ratio of shear stress to shear strain within elastic limit.

$$G = \frac{\tau}{\phi}$$

Bulk Modulus (K)

Ratio of direct stress to volumetric strain.

$$K = \frac{\sigma}{\varepsilon_v}$$

Poisson's Ratio (ν)

Ratio of lateral strain to longitudinal strain.

$$\nu = -\frac{\text{Lateral Strain}}{\text{Longitudinal Strain}}$$

3. Factor of Safety Pages 5-6

Yield Point: Point beyond which material extends considerably without increase in load.

Ultimate Stress: Maximum stress before failure. Also called maximum strength or tensile strength.

Fracture Point: Point where material breaks.

Working Stress: Stress used in design, well within elastic limit.

$$\text{F.O.S} = \frac{\text{Ultimate Stress}}{\text{Working Stress}}$$

4. Bars of Uniform Section Pages 7-8

Example 1

A bar 1000mm long and 20mm × 20mm in cross-section is subjected to axial pull of 40kN. If E = 80 GN/m², calculate elongation.

Solution:

$A = 20 \times 20 = 400$ mm², $L = 1000$ mm, $F = 40 \times 10^3$ N

$$\Delta L = \frac{FL}{AE} = \frac{40 \times 10^3 \times 1000}{400 \times 80 \times 10^3} = 1.25 \text{ mm}$$

5. Hollow Cylinder Page 9

A hollow cast iron cylinder 4m long, 300mm outer dia, 50mm thick, carries axial load 500kN. Find stress, contraction, strain energy. E = 150 GPa

Solution:

$D = 300$mm, $t = 50$mm → $d = 200$mm

$A = \frac{\pi}{4}(300^2 - 200^2) = 39,270$ mm²

$$\sigma = \frac{500 \times 10^3}{39270} = 12.73 \text{ N/mm²}$$
$$\Delta L = \frac{500 \times 10^3 \times 4000}{39270 \times 150 \times 10^3} = 0.34 \text{ mm}$$

6. Bars of Varying Cross-Section Pages 10-11

For stepped bar with sections $L_1, A_1$ and $L_2, A_2$:

$$\Delta L = \frac{PL_1}{A_1E} + \frac{PL_2}{A_2E}$$

General: $\Delta L = \frac{1}{AE} \sum FL$

8. Composite Bars Pages 12-14

For composite bar with materials 1 and 2:

$$F_1 = \frac{F A_1 E_1}{A_1 E_1 + A_2 E_2} \quad , \quad F_2 = \frac{F A_2 E_2}{A_1 E_1 + A_2 E_2}$$

Extension: $\Delta L = \frac{F_1 L}{A_1 E_1} = \frac{F_2 L}{A_2 E_2}$

9. R.C.C Square Columns Pages 14-16

Modular Ratio: $m = \frac{E_s}{E_c}$

Stress relation: $\sigma_s = m \sigma_c$

Load capacity: $P = A_c \sigma_c + A_s \sigma_s$

R.C.C Square Column: 300×300mm with 4×20mm dia bars

Example: 300×300mm Column

4 bars of 20mm dia, $m = 18$, $\sigma_c = 6$ N/mm²

$A_s = 1256.6$ mm², $A_c = 88743.4$ mm²

$$P = 88743.4 \times 6 + 1256.6 \times 18 \times 6 = 668.14 \text{ kN}$$

10. R.C.C Circular Columns Page 17

R.C.C Circular Column: 400mm dia with 5×20mm bars

Example: 400mm dia, 5 bars 20mm dia

$m = 18$, $\sigma_c \leq 5$ N/mm², $\sigma_s \leq 120$ N/mm²

Concrete governs: $\sigma_s = 18 \times 5 = 90$ N/mm²

$$P = 124092.9 \times 5 + 1570.8 \times 90 = 761.84 \text{ kN}$$

11. Thermal Stress & Strain Pages 18-19

Free expansion: $\Delta L = L \alpha t$

Thermal strain: $\varepsilon = \alpha t$

If restrained: $\sigma = E \alpha t$

Thermal force: $F = EA\alpha t$

4-Step Method

  1. Calculate free expansion: $\Delta L = L\alpha t$
  2. Find force to restore original length
  3. Calculate stress: $\sigma = E\varepsilon$
  4. Determine if tensile or compressive

Example: Aluminum bar

Fixed ends, heated 80°C, $E = 80$ kN/mm², $\alpha = 24 \times 10^{-6}$

$$\sigma = 80 \times 10^3 \times 80 \times 24 \times 10^{-6} = 153.6 \text{ N/mm² compressive}$$

12. Thermal Stress - Fixed Ends Page 20

Bar with both ends fixed. Temperature rise causes compressive reaction forces.

Example: Brass rod

20m long, fixed ends, $\sigma \leq 76.5$ N/mm², find max temp rise

$\alpha = 17 \times 10^{-6}$, $E = 90$ kN/mm²

$$t = \frac{76.5}{17 \times 10^{-6} \times 90 \times 10^3} = 50°C$$

13. Thermal Stress - Stepped Bars Page 21

For stepped bar with rigid supports:

$$\Delta L_{free} = L\alpha t = \Delta L_P = \frac{PL_1}{A_1E} + \frac{PL_2}{A_2E}$$

Steel rod: $L_1=500$mm, $A_1=400$mm², $L_2=300$mm, $A_2=600$mm², $t=15$K

$$P = 16457 \text{ N}, \quad \sigma_1 = 41.14 \text{ N/mm²}, \quad \sigma_2 = 27.43 \text{ N/mm²}$$

14. Composite Thermal Stress Pages 22-25

Theory: Brass + Steel

$\alpha_{brass} > \alpha_{steel}$ → Brass compressed, Steel in tension

Equilibrium: $\sigma_b A_b = \sigma_s A_s$

Compatibility: $\alpha_b t - \frac{\sigma_b}{E_b} = \alpha_s t + \frac{\sigma_s}{E_s}$

Example 1: Steel + Aluminum Sandwich Page 24

Aluminum in compression, Steel in tension when heated. $\alpha_{al} > \alpha_s$

Steel: 20×3mm, Al: 2×20×6mm, $t=50$°C

$E_s=200$, $\alpha_s=12\times10^{-6}$, $E_{al}=80$, $\alpha_{al}=24\times10^{-6}$

$$\sigma_{al} = 32 \text{ N/mm² compressive}$$
$$\sigma_s = 128 \text{ N/mm² tensile}$$

Example 2: Gun Metal Rod + Steel Tube Page 25

GM rod in compression, Steel tube in tension. Bolted ends force equal displacement.

GM: 20mm dia, Steel: 25mm ID, 30mm OD, $t=200$°F

$E_s=200$, $\alpha_s=6\times10^{-6}$, $E_{gm}=100$, $\alpha_{gm}=10\times10^{-6}$

$$\sigma_{gm} = 72.73 \text{ N/mm² compressive}$$
$$\sigma_s = 105.8 \text{ N/mm² tensile}$$
